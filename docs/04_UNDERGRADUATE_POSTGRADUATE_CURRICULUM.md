## UNDERGRADUATE YEAR 1 - Foundation Through Practice

**Your 6-Month AI Journey - One Month at a Time**

### MONTH 1: AI DISCOVER - See • Understand • Explore
**Area: AI Basics**

#### Topic 1: AI Demystified
**What you'll understand:**
- AI = Machines doing tasks that normally need human intelligence
- Three circles: AI ⊃ Machine Learning ⊃ Deep Learning
- Today's AI is narrow (great at ONE thing, not everything)
- Four learning styles: Supervised, Unsupervised, Reinforcement, Self-supervised

**Real-world examples:**
- Supervised = Email learning spam from labeled examples (spam/not spam)
- Unsupervised = Netflix grouping movies you didn't know were similar
- Reinforcement = Game AI learning by trial and error (like learning to ride bike)
- Self-supervised = ChatGPT predicting next word from billions of sentences

**Daily life connections:**
- Your phone's face unlock = Supervised learning from your face photos
- Spotify Discover Weekly = Unsupervised clustering of similar songs
- Google Maps avoiding traffic = Reinforcement learning from millions of routes
- Autocorrect on keyboard = Self-supervised learning from text patterns

**Activities:**
- Classify 20 things around you into AI/Not AI
- Track every AI interaction in one day (will find 50+!)
- Explain AI to a 10-year-old family member

#### Topic 2: The Intelligence Behind Machines
**What you'll understand:**
- Search algorithms (finding best path, like Google Maps)
- Decision trees (asking yes/no questions until answer found)
- Regression (predicting numbers like house prices)
- Neural networks (layers learning progressively complex patterns)

**Real-world examples:**
- A* search = How navigation apps find fastest route
- Decision tree = How banks approve loans (income? credit score? employment?)
- Linear regression = Predicting your Uber fare based on distance
- Neural network = Face ID recognizing you at different angles/lighting

**Daily life connections:**
- Choosing fastest route to college (search algorithm in your head)
- Doctor diagnosing illness (decision tree of symptoms)
- Estimating movie ticket sales from marketing spend (regression)
- Your brain recognizing friend's voice on phone (neural network)

**Activities:**
- Draw decision tree for "Should I carry umbrella?"
- Calculate simple regression: study hours vs exam marks
- Trace how you recognize a face (edges → features → identity)

### MONTH 2: AI CONNECT - Think • Instruct • Code
**Area: Communicate with AI**

#### Topic 1: Python + AI Starter (Practical Basics Only)
**What you'll understand:**
- Python = Most popular AI language (readable, simple)
- NumPy = Fast number crunching (Excel on steroids)
- pandas = Data tables (like spreadsheets, but programmable)
- scikit-learn = Ready-made AI models

**Real-world examples:**
- NumPy = Financial calculations for millions of transactions instantly
- pandas = Analyzing company sales data across 10 years
- scikit-learn = Training spam filter without building from scratch
- Jupyter notebooks = Lab journal where code, notes, graphs stay together

**Daily life connections:**
- Excel formulas = NumPy operations (but 1000x faster)
- Filtering/sorting Excel = pandas operations
- Using Instagram filters = Using pre-built tools (like scikit-learn)
- Recipe with photos = Jupyter notebook (steps + results together)

**Activities:**
- Load CSV of your expenses, find highest spending category
- Calculate average of list of numbers (your way vs NumPy way)
- Use pre-built model to classify images (no code from scratch)

#### Topic 2: Prompt with Purpose
**What you'll understand:**
- Good prompts = Clear goal + Audience + Context + Constraints
- Patterns: Few-shot (examples), Chain-of-thought (step-by-step), Persona (role-play)
- Structured output (JSON) for machine-readable results
- Testing prompts systematically (like A/B testing)

**Real-world examples:**
- Bad prompt: "Explain AI" (vague, generic result)
- Good prompt: "Explain AI to a 60-year-old farmer in 100 words with 2 examples from agriculture"
- Chain-of-thought: "Solve this math step-by-step, showing each calculation"
- Structured output: "Return 5 movie suggestions as JSON with title, year, genre"

**Daily life connections:**
- Asking clear questions = Getting good answers (life lesson!)
- Giving detailed instructions to delivery person = Better results
- Doctor asking specific symptoms = Accurate diagnosis
- Teacher giving clear assignment instructions = Better student work

**Activities:**
- Transform 5 vague prompts into purposeful ones
- Test same question with 3 different prompts, compare
- Create your personal "Prompting Cheat Sheet"

**What you'll understand:**
- Core principles: Fairness, Transparency, Accountability, Safety, Privacy
- Bias enters through data, design, deployment

**Real-world examples:**

- Transparency: Users should know AI is making decisions
- Accountability: Someone responsible when AI fails
- Bias: Hiring AI trained on mostly male resumes discriminates

**Daily life connections:**

#### Topic 2: Data Privacy Matters
**What you'll understand:**
- Personal data = Any info identifying you (directly or indirectly)
- India's DPDP Act: Consent, minimization, security, rights
- Pseudonymization vs Anonymization
- Privacy by design (build privacy in from start)

**Real-world examples:**
- Name + Phone = Personal data (obvious)
- Age + Pin code + College = Can identify you too!
- DPDP Act = Rules for how companies handle your data
- Pseudonymization = Replacing name with ID (reversible)
- Anonymization = Making truly unidentifiable (hard to do right)

**Daily life connections:**
- App asking unnecessary permissions (why does flashlight need contacts?)

- Data breach news (someone's data stolen, can happen to you)
- Privacy settings (taking control of your data)

**Activities:**
- Audit apps on phone, revoke unnecessary permissions

- Create "Privacy Protection Guide" for family
