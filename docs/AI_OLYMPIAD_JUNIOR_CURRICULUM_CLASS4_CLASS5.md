## 3. Class 4 Complete Curriculum Specification (12 Lessons)

### CHAPTER 1: AI BASICS

#### Lesson 1: Where Is AI Hiding?
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 4, Chapter 1, Lesson 1
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Spot AI embedded quietly in everyday devices (cameras, keyboards, maps, streaming apps).
  2. Distinguish between normal machines (fixed switches) and AI machines (learn from data and patterns).
  3. Understand that data is the essential "food" that AI systems need to learn.
- **Opening Mystery:** Robo-Pi notices glowing invisible helper signals across modern homes. Why does a smartphone camera draw a box around faces, while a ceiling fan just spins when switched on?
- **Concept Explanation:** Normal tools (rulers, pencils, simple fans) follow fixed physical mechanisms. AI tools (face unlock, autocomplete keyboards, streaming recommendation feeds) collect sensory data, identify patterns, and adapt dynamically.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Sensor Clues:* Microphones and camera sensors collect raw digital pixels and sound waves.
  2. *Pattern Recognition:* Computer vision algorithms compare input pixels against learned facial features.
  3. *Smart Action:* The phone softly blurs the background in portrait mode or predicts the next word.
- **Unique Game:** **AI Detective: The Invisible Signal Hunt**
  - *Scenario:* Robo-Pi scans 5 rooms in Mystery Mansion using a Digital Magnifier.
  - *Rules:* Tag objects as "AI FOUND!" (if they learn or adapt) or "NORMAL TOOL" (if they follow manual switches).
  - *Progression:* Living Room (Voice Remote vs. Clock) → Kitchen (Robot Vacuum vs. Broom) → Study Desk (Face Unlock vs. Ruler).
  - *Score & Badge:* 300 points, *Certified AI Detective Badge 🕵️‍♂️*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "The Home AI Detective Mission" — Inspect 4 household appliances with an adult and create a two-column classification chart.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Which item uses AI? (A: Wooden ruler, B: Face-unlock camera [Correct], C: Plain ceiling fan, D: Graphite pencil). *Explanation: Face-unlock uses computer vision pattern recognition.*
  2. *Stage 2:* Camera autofocus draws a box around a pet's face. Which AI field is this? (Computer Vision [Correct]). *Explanation: Computer vision gives machines the ability to see and locate objects.*
  3. *Championship:* An AI trained only on orange indoor cats guesses "Squirrel" on a black outdoor cat. Why? (Incomplete, biased training data [Correct]). *Explanation: AI cannot recognize patterns it was never taught.*
- **Reflection & Safety:** Always ask parents before enabling camera or location permissions in mobile apps.
- **Completion Criteria:** Spot all 3 AI devices in the mansion without false alarms; master 3 Olympiad questions.

#### Lesson 2: Meet the Smart Machines
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 4, Chapter 1, Lesson 2
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Master the 3-step cycle of smart machines: SENSE (sensors), THINK (AI brain), and ACT (motors and speakers).
  2. Explore autonomous rovers, delivery drones, and self-driving shuttles.
  3. Understand why robots excel at tasks that are dull, dirty, dangerous, or located in extreme environments.
- **Opening Mystery:** Space Rover Pi is exploring Mars, where radio signals from Earth take 15 minutes to arrive. If a jagged crater appears ahead, how does the rover stop itself from crashing?
- **Concept Explanation:** Smart machines operate via the Sense-Think-Act loop. Sensors (LiDAR, cameras, ultrasonic) act as eyes; computers evaluate rules and neural networks; motors and robotic arms execute physical motions.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Sense:* Ultrasonic sensors measure distance to obstacles (e.g. 15 cm).
  2. *Think:* The microchip calculates: `IF distance < 20cm THEN decision = "TURN_LEFT"`.
  3. *Act:* Wheel motors pivot 90 degrees clockwise to navigate safely around obstacles.
- **Unique Game:** **Sense-Think-Act Mission Commander**
  - *Scenario:* Command Rover Pi through a Martian volcanic maze with lava fissures, crystal rocks, and dust storms.
  - *Rules:* Pair the correct sensor (Infrared/LiDAR/Camera) to the proper thinking rule and motor actuator.
  - *Progression:* Level 1 (Fissure Avoidance) → Level 2 (Crystal Sample Collection) → Level 3 (Dust Storm Lockdown).
  - *Score & Badge:* 350 points, *Autonomous Systems Commander Badge 🦾*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "Automatic Pet Feeder Blueprint" — Draw and label Sense (motion sensor), Think (timer chip), and Act (dispenser motor) components.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Which step in a robot vacuum corresponds to "ACT"? (Motors spinning wheels to turn [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* What role does a barcode laser play in a warehouse robot? (Sense [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* What is Sensor Fusion? (Combining camera and LiDAR data to make reliable, safe navigation decisions [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Autonomous vehicles must always feature fail-safe stops whenever sensors become obscured by mud or heavy rain.
- **Completion Criteria:** Successfully navigate Rover Pi through all 3 Martian hazard zones.

### CHAPTER 2: COMMUNICATE WITH AI

#### Lesson 1: Say It Clearly!
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 4, Chapter 2, Lesson 1
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Understand why vague words confuse AI systems.
  2. Master precision: specifying exact parameters, subjects, colors, and constraints.
  3. Learn how to transform fuzzy commands into clear instructions.
- **Opening Mystery:** Chef Bot was asked to "make a nice snack", and it brought a raw onion on top of a cookie! Why did Chef Bot make such a terrible snack?
- **Concept Explanation:** Humans use tone, gestures, and implied common sense. Computers only understand explicit words. If instructions lack specifics, the computer must guess from millions of possibilities.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Subject (WHO/WHAT):* Specify exact items ("A solar-powered lunar rover").
  2. *Details (WHERE/HOW):* Add attributes ("Silver and gold, in a lunar crater, children's book style").
  3. *Constraints:* Establish output limits ("3 bullet points, under 50 words").
- **Unique Game:** **Chef Bot Recipe Precision Challenge**
  - *Scenario:* Refine vague kitchen orders into exact parameter cards to bake a Strawberry Birthday Cake.
  - *Rules:* Replace fuzzy words ("some flour", "hot oven") with exact measurements ("250g wheat flour", "180°C for 25 minutes").
  - *Score & Badge:* 340 points, *Master Clarifier Chef Badge 👨‍🍳*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "The Blindfold Sandwich Simulator" — Direct a partner who acts as a literal robot with zero common sense.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Identify the ambiguous command: (A: Turn 90°, B: Move forward 5m, C: Move a little bit over there [Correct], D: Stop at 10cm).
  2. *Stage 2:* Which prompt produces the best science diagram? (A detailed prompt with labeled parts and style [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* What is the best response when an AI gives inaccurate dinosaur data? (Refine prompt with specific parameters and request authoritative citations [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** AI can help brainstorm, but human creators must add personal heart and verified facts.
- **Completion Criteria:** Complete 3 recipe refinements without burning the virtual bakery cake.

#### Lesson 2: Mission: Instructions
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 4, Chapter 2, Lesson 2
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Understand that an algorithm is an ordered step-by-step recipe to complete a task.
  2. Learn why chronological sequence is critical for computers.
  3. Identify and debug out-of-order instruction errors (bugs).
- **Opening Mystery:** Rover Pi is on a crater ledge. If it executes Step 3 (Drive Forward) before Step 2 (Lower Bridge), it falls into the crater! Why does sequence matter so much?
- **Concept Explanation:** Computers execute instructions strictly from top to bottom. Out-of-order steps create bugs.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Deconstruct:* Break big missions into atomic steps.
  2. *Sequence:* Arrange in chronological dependency order.
  3. *Condition (IF-THEN):* Add safety checks (`IF obstacle THEN detour`).
- **Unique Game:** **Rover Launch Sequence Programmer**
  - *Scenario:* Reorder 5 scrambled launch blocks (Touchdown, Deploy Wings, Scan Terrain, Drive, Drill).
  - *Rules:* Solar wings must deploy before driving; terrain scanning must precede forward movement.
  - *Score & Badge:* 350 points, *Algorithm Architect Badge 📐*.
- **Practical Challenge:** Author a 5-step morning routine algorithm with one IF-THEN weather condition.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Definition of algorithm: (Step-by-step list of instructions to complete a task [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Identify out-of-order steps in electric kettle boiling: (Plugging in must precede boiling [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Why does [Move 1m → Scan] cause accidents? (The robot drives blind during the 1-meter movement interval [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Safety checks in autonomous robotics must always take precedence over speed.
- **Completion Criteria:** Successfully reorder the Martian launch timeline with 0 sequencing bugs.

### CHAPTER 6: RESPONSIBLE AI

- **Opening Mystery:** An online game mascot offers 1,000 free bonus diamonds if you enter your home address and mother's phone number! Should you enter it?
- **Concept Explanation:** Personal data typed online can be stored, analyzed, or leaked. Protect your digital footprint by keeping PII strictly confidential.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Recognize PII:* Identify names, addresses, phone numbers, and passwords.
  2. *Apply Shield:* Ask: "Is this general knowledge or my private real-world identity?".
  3. *Consult Adult:* Involve parents whenever an app asks for sensitive data.
- **Unique Game:** **Privacy Vault Shield: Safe vs Secret Sieve**
  - *Scenario:* Guard the Digital Vault against 6 incoming data packets.
  - *Rules:* Click Green (PASS) for general science queries; Slam Red Shield (LOCK) on private secrets.
  - *Score & Badge:* 350 points, *Cyber Vault Guardian Badge 🛡️*.
- **Practical Challenge:** Make a "Never Share Online" door hanger for your study desk listing the 4 locked secrets.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Which item is safe to type into a chatbot? (Science question about why the sky is blue [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Why should you never upload a school ID card online? (Contains real name, photo, and student ID that can be stolen [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* What hardware feature protects smart speakers from recording private discussions? (Hardware microphone mute switch [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Chatbots are computer programs, not human friends; they cannot keep real-world secrets.
- **Completion Criteria:** Successfully filter all 6 data packets with 0 privacy leaks.

#### Lesson 2: AI: Right or Wrong?
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 4, Chapter 6, Lesson 2
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Understand why AI makes mistakes: incomplete data, confusing patterns, and hallucinations.
  2. Recognize that AI often sounds polite and confident even when completely wrong.
  3. Master the STOP, THINK, CHECK traffic light verification protocol.
- **Opening Mystery:** Why did an advanced AI image model confuse photos of blueberry muffins with fluffy Chihuahua puppies?
- **Concept Explanation:** AI matches mathematical pixel patterns rather than understanding what an animal or pastry is. Dark blueberries look mathematically identical to dark puppy eyes and noses.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Recognize Risk:* Understand that confident tone does not equal truth.
  2. *Deploy Traffic Light:* STOP (don't copy), THINK (does this make sense?), CHECK (verify in books).
  3. *Cross-Check:* Verify facts in school encyclopedias.
- **Unique Game:** **Fact-Check Detective: Spot the Hallucination**
  - *Scenario:* Audit 4 science statements from ChatterBot 2.0 using the Golden Encyclopedia.
  - *Claims:* Penguins swim/can't fly (True); Great Wall made of chocolate (Hallucination); Bees dance (True); Spiders have 6 legs (Hallucination).
  - *Score & Badge:* 340 points, *Master Truth Detective Badge 🔍*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "The AI Mistake Detective Hunt" — Audit 5 statements with a teacher, marking Right or Wrong with verified sources.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* What is an AI Hallucination? (When an AI makes up false, fabricated information while presenting it as fact [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Why did a dog model guess "Polar Bear" on snow? (Trained only on grass backgrounds; snow confused its prediction [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Most responsible way to study with AI: (Use AI to explain concepts, then cross-check every key fact with official textbooks [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Never take medicine or health advice from a chatbot; always consult a licensed doctor and your parents.
- **Completion Criteria:** Successfully identify both hallucinated statements and provide correct proof.

## 4. Class 5 Complete Curriculum Specification (12 Lessons)

### CHAPTER 1: AI BASICS

#### Lesson 1: The Secret Behind AI
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 5, Chapter 1, Lesson 1
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Discover the secret of AI: learning patterns from thousands of labeled examples rather than rigid hand-coded rules.
  2. Understand the 3 pillars of Machine Learning: Data, Patterns, and Practice.
  3. Explore Artificial Neural Networks: Input Layer → Hidden Thinking Layers with adjustable Weights → Output Layer.
- **Opening Mystery:** How can a computer recognize a cat when no programmer ever wrote code defining every single cat fur and whisker?
- **Concept Explanation:** Traditional software follows fixed `IF-THEN` rules written by humans. Machine Learning feeds 50,000 photos into a neural network, allowing the system to discover its own mathematical patterns.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Ingest Data:* Feed labeled photos into input neurons.
  2. *Forward Pass:* Signals multiply across hidden layers by connection weights.
  3. *Backpropagation:* Errors adjust connection weights slightly to reduce mistakes.
- **Unique Game:** **Neural Net Neuron Linker & Weight Tuner**
  - *Scenario:* Tune connection weights on a 3-layer neural network classifying Kittens vs. Puppies.
  - *Rules:* Calibrate Pointy Ear weight (+0.85), Snout Curvature weight, and bias threshold.
  - *Score & Badge:* 360 points, *Neural Network Architect Badge 🧠*.
- **Practical Challenge:** Draw and trace a 3-layer neural network classifying apples vs. oranges with calculated connection flows.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* What happens when a neural net "learns"? (Mathematical connection weights between neurons are adjusted [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Why were neural networks inspired by biology? (Scientists observed how interconnected brain neurons learn from experience [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Why are hidden layers called "hidden"? (They perform intermediate mathematical processing between inputs and outputs [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Complex neural networks are often "black boxes"; high-stakes applications require human explainability.
- **Completion Criteria:** Tune weights across 3 epochs to achieve 95%+ classification accuracy.

#### Lesson 2: Learning Machines
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 5, Chapter 1, Lesson 2
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Master the 5-step ML cycle: 1. Collect Data → 2. Find Patterns → 3. Predict → 4. Get Feedback → 5. Adjust & Get Smarter.
  2. Understand the formula: Learning = Guess, Check, Fix, Repeat.
  3. Distinguish between Training Data (practice worksheets) and Test Data (unseen exams).
- **Opening Mystery:** When an AI model starts learning, it scores 50% wrong. After 10,000 feedback rounds, it achieves 99% accuracy. How does it improve without feelings?
- **Concept Explanation:** Machine learning is an iterative optimization loop. Evaluating models on held-out test data proves whether they learned generalized concepts rather than merely memorizing practice examples.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Partition:* Split dataset into 80% Training and 20% Testing.
  2. *Epoch Loop:* Run training batches; loss decreases over time.
  3. *Validate:* Evaluate accuracy on unseen test data.
- **Unique Game:** **Data Training Gym: Train the Classifier**
  - *Scenario:* Train "PixelBot" to sort apples from tomatoes on a grocery conveyor belt.
  - *Rules:* Balance sample counts, trigger backpropagation, and evaluate on mystery produce.
  - *Score & Badge:* 370 points, *Machine Learning Trainer Badge 🏋️‍♂️*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "Paper Flashcard ML Experiment" — Simulate the 5-step loop with a partner using cards and token counters.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Role of Feedback in machine learning: (Informs the model whether its guess was right so it can adjust weights [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Why sunny-road car models fail in snow: (Training dataset lacked snow diversity, creating blind spots [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Model scores 100% on training data but 52% on test data: (Overfitting: memorized training data instead of general patterns [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** "Garbage In, Garbage Out": Poor or biased training data leads directly to poor, biased real-world outputs.
- **Completion Criteria:** Achieve 98% accuracy on the unseen mystery produce evaluation.

### CHAPTER 2: COMMUNICATE WITH AI

#### Lesson 1: Crack the Logic!
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 5, Chapter 2, Lesson 1
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Understand Boolean logic states: strictly TRUE or FALSE.
  2. Master logic gates: AND (all true), OR (at least one true), and NOT (inverts truth).
  3. Construct branching decision trees and conditional IF-THEN-ELSE statements.
- **Opening Mystery:** An automated greenhouse watered the plants while heavy rain was already pouring outside! What logical bug caused this waste of water?
- **Concept Explanation:** Computers execute decisions through Boolean algebra. The greenhouse checked `IF soil_dry: water()`, forgetting to include `AND NOT raining_outside`.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Define States:* Sensors report binary values (True/False).
  2. *Combine Gates:* Link criteria with `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`.
  3. *Branch:* Execute distinct pathways with `IF-THEN-ELSE`.
- **Unique Game:** **Logic Gate Maze: Automated Greenhouse**
  - *Scenario:* Wire Boolean logic circuits for climate pumps in the Lunar Biosphere Greenhouse.
  - *Gates:* Sprinkler → `[Soil Moisture < 20%] AND [NOT Raining]`; Shade → `[Sun > 80%] OR [Temp > 32°C]`.
  - *Score & Badge:* 370 points, *Logic Gate Master Badge ⚡*.
- **Practical Challenge:** Map a complete "What to Wear" decision tree with temperature and rain branching.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Evaluate `(False OR True)`: (True [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Automated school bell on Sunday at 13:00: (Bell does not ring because `is_weekend == False` evaluates to False [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Evaluate `NOT (1 AND 1)`: (0 / False [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** Every automated logical system must include a manual emergency override to protect human lives.
- **Completion Criteria:** Successfully wire all 3 environmental logic gate circuits.

- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 5, Chapter 2, Lesson 2

- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Assemble 4 Blocks:* Combine persona, task, context, and format.
  2. *Review Output:* Check constraints, word limits, and tone.
  3. *Iterative Refinement:* Steer the model with targeted follow-up prompts.
- **Unique Game:** **Prompt Recipe Kitchen: 4-Block Builder**
  - *Scenario:* Snap together WHO, WHAT, DETAILS, and HOW ingredient cards for 3 client orders.
  - *Orders:* History roleplay dialog, Fractions chef guide, Ocean eco-poem.
  - *Score & Badge:* 360 points, *Prompt Engineering Specialist Badge 🧑‍🍳*.
- **Practical Challenge:** Transform 3 vague prompts into precision 4-block master commands.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* What does the WHO block specify? (The persona or expert role the AI should adopt [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Identify the DETAILS block in a water cycle prompt: ("Explain evaporation, condensation, and precipitation for 5th graders" [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Why did a prompt produce 350 words of university math instead of a 100-word summary? (Lacked a HOW block specifying length and reading level [Correct]).

- **Completion Criteria:** Achieve a 90%+ score across all 3 client prompt recipes.

### CHAPTER 6: RESPONSIBLE AI

#### Lesson 1: Can AI Be Wrong?
- **Syllabus Reference:** Class 5, Chapter 6, Lesson 1
- **Learning Objectives:**
  1. Discover the root causes of AI errors: dataset bias, lack of common sense, and confident hallucinations.
  2. Understand the "Orange Cat vs. Black Cat" problem.
  3. Learn how to audit datasets for fairness, representation, and edge cases.
- **Opening Mystery:** An image recognition model trained on 20,000 orange cats looked at a sleek black panther and called it a "Black Car"! Why did the machine fail?
- **Concept Explanation:** AI models depend entirely on statistical patterns in their training data. Homogeneous datasets create blind spots. Generating models can hallucinate plausible-sounding falsehoods without hesitation.
- **Guided Discovery Steps:**
  1. *Audit Distribution:* Check class frequencies for underrepresented groups.
  2. *Rebalance:* Inject diverse examples and augment data.
  3. *Stress-Test:* Test models on difficult lighting and edge cases.
- **Unique Game:** **Dataset Bias & Hallucination Buster**
  - *Scenario:* Audit a wildlife camera model with high errors during twilight hours.
  - *Rules:* Rebalance skewed class distributions and catch 3 hallucinated animal tags.
  - *Score & Badge:* 380 points, *AI Fairness Auditor Badge ⚖️*.
- **Practical Challenge:** "Dataset Bias Audit Checklist" — Audit a 10,000-bird photo collection and identify 3 representation gaps.
- **Olympiad Assessment:**
  1. *Stage 1:* Meaning of the "Orange Cat Dilemma": (Dataset bias where an AI trained on narrow examples fails on real-world diversity [Correct]).
  2. *Stage 2:* Chatbot claiming Mars Rover Club won the 2045 World Cup: (Hallucination: generated fictional text instead of recognizing 2045 is in the future [Correct]).
  3. *Championship:* Hiring tool rejecting resumes containing "Women's": (Historical dataset bias: model learned unfair historical patterns [Correct]).
- **Reflection & Safety:** AI learns historical human patterns, including past prejudices; humans must actively correct bias.
- **Completion Criteria:** Eliminate class bias across all 3 animal categories and resolve hallucinations.
