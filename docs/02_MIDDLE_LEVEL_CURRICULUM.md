# MIDDLE LEVEL CURRICULUM - Classes 6-8
## Simple AI Learning Through Everyday Examples (Ages 11-14)

**Philosophy:** NO actual coding teaching - only real-world overviews and everyday examples. Everything explained through daily life activities that anyone can understand.

---

## CLASS 6 - AI in My Daily Life

### Chapter 1: AI Basics
**Month 1-2**

#### Lesson 1: How Machines Get Smart
**What students learn:**
- Machines learn like we learn - by seeing many examples
- Example: You learned to recognize mangoes by seeing many mangoes
- AI learns to recognize cats by seeing thousands of cat photos
- Simple explanation: "AI is like teaching a baby - show it many times, it remembers the pattern"

**Real-life connections:**
- Your phone's keyboard predicting next word (it saw millions of messages)
- Instagram knowing which reels you'll like (it watched what you watched before)
- Google Photos grouping all photos of your friend together

**Activities:**
- Show 10 apple pictures, ask what's common
- "Teach" a friend to recognize your favorite snack by showing examples
- Discuss: How did you learn to ride a bicycle? (practice = learning)

#### Lesson 2: Learning from Examples
**What students learn:**
- Good examples help machines learn better
- Bad examples confuse machines
- More examples = smarter machine
- Example: If you only show AI pictures of white cats, it won't recognize black cats

**Real-life connections:**
- Learning math by solving many problems
- Your mom's cooking getting better with practice
- Sports players watching match videos to improve

**Activities:**
- Collect 20 pictures of different dogs - what makes them all "dogs"?
- Try teaching a concept with only 3 examples vs 20 examples
- Create a "good example vs bad example" poster

### Chapter 2: Communicate with AI
**Month 3-4**

#### Lesson 1: Think → Plan → Code
**What students learn:**
- Before telling a machine what to do, you must think step-by-step
- Breaking big tasks into small steps
- Example: Making tea = boil water + add tea powder + add sugar + add milk
- NO actual coding - just understanding the thinking process

**Real-life connections:**
- Your morning routine (wake up → brush → bath → breakfast)
- Recipe for making sandwich (step 1, step 2, step 3...)
- Directions to reach school from home

**Activities:**
- Write steps to make your favorite dish
- Break down "getting ready for school" into 15 steps
- Play "Robot Game" - give step-by-step instructions to a friend

#### Lesson 2: Coding Made Friendly (Overview Only - NO Teaching Code)
**What students learn:**
- Coding is just giving step-by-step instructions in a language machines understand
- Like how we speak Hindi/English, machines understand Python
- Example: "Alexa, play music" = instruction in English, machine understands it
- We're NOT learning to write code - just understanding what it is

**Real-life connections:**
- TV remote buttons (each button = an instruction)
- Traffic signals (red/yellow/green = instructions to drivers)
- Recipe book (instructions for cooking)

**Activities:**
- Give instructions for a paper airplane (step-by-step)
- Play "Simon Says" (following exact instructions)
- Draw a picture by following only verbal instructions

### Chapter 6: Responsible AI
**Month 11-12**

#### Lesson 1: Protect Your Data
**What students learn:**
- Personal information = name, phone, address, school name, photos
- Never share personal info online
- Apps asking for too many permissions (why does a game need your contacts?)
- Strong passwords (mix of letters, numbers, symbols)

**Real-life connections:**
- Not telling strangers where you live
- Not accepting friend requests from unknown people
- Asking parents before downloading apps

**Activities:**
- Create a "Safe vs Unsafe" chart for online activities
- Make a strong password for a pretend account
- Role-play: saying NO to sharing personal info

#### Lesson 2: Think Before You Trust
**What students learn:**
- Not everything online is true (fake news, edited photos)
- AI can create fake videos (deepfakes)
- Always check: Is this from a trusted source?
- If something looks too good to be true, it probably is

**Real-life connections:**
- "You won a prize!" messages that are fake
- Photos that look real but are AI-generated
- Rumors spreading on WhatsApp

**Activities:**
- Find 3 fake news examples
- Spot the difference: real vs AI-generated image
- Create a "Think Before You Share" poster

**CLASS 6 SUMMARY:**
Students understand AI through daily examples, see it in roads and farms, know basic online safety, and feel confident about future careers!

## CLASS 8 - AI for Real Solutions

### Chapter 1: AI Basics
**Month 1-2**

#### Lesson 1: How AI Makes Choices
**What students learn:**
- AI makes decisions by comparing with past examples
- Example: Spam filter deciding if email is spam or not
- Decision trees = asking yes/no questions until you reach an answer
- Simple explanation: Like playing 20 questions game

**Real-life connections:**
- Doctor diagnosing illness (asking symptoms one by one)
- Choosing what to wear (hot? cold? rainy?)
- Deciding which route to take (traffic? distance?)

**Activities:**
- Create a decision tree for "What should I eat?"
- Play 20 questions game
- Map out your morning routine as yes/no choices

#### Lesson 2: Learning from Data (Deeper Look)
**What students learn:**
- Supervised learning = learning with a teacher (labeled examples)
- Unsupervised learning = finding patterns without a teacher
- Example: Supervised = learning math from a teacher; Unsupervised = discovering your favorite music genre
- Reinforcement learning = learning by trial and error (like learning to ride a bike)

**Real-life connections:**
- Learning to cook with mom watching (supervised)
- Organizing your closet by yourself (unsupervised)
- Playing video games and improving (reinforcement)

**Activities:**
- Classify 20 objects with given categories (supervised)
- Group 30 random items by your own logic (unsupervised)
- Play a game and track your improvement (reinforcement)

### Chapter 6: Responsible AI
**Month 11-12**

#### Lesson 1: Deepfake Alert!
**What students learn:**
- Deepfakes = hyper-realistic fake videos/audio created by AI
- Dangers: Spreading false information, impersonation, fraud
- How to detect: Look for unnatural movements, check source
- Legal and ethical issues

**Real-life connections:**
- Fake celebrity videos saying things they never said
- Voice clones used in scams (pretending to be relatives)
- Political deepfakes during elections

**Activities:**
- Watch real vs deepfake examples, spot differences
- Research one major deepfake incident
- Create a "Spot the Deepfake" guide

#### Lesson 2: Your Data, Your Right
**What students learn:**
- What data apps collect (location, contacts, photos, browsing history)
- Why companies want your data (targeted ads, selling to others)
- Your rights: Know what's collected, delete your data, say no
- Privacy settings on popular apps

**Real-life connections:**
- Apps asking for camera/microphone access
- Targeted ads showing exactly what you searched for
- Free apps that make money by selling your data

**Activities:**
- Audit permissions on your phone apps
- Create a "Privacy Protection Checklist"
- Write a letter to an app company asking about data usage
